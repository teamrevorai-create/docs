The search prompt is the most important input in **Targets**. The more clearly it describes the companies you want, the more of your results will be a real fit. This guide shows what to include and how to write a prompt that finds companies ready to buy.

## What to include

A good prompt describes two things: who your ideal customer is (your ICP) and what shows they may need your product right now (buying signals).

| **Part** | **What it describes** | **Example** |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Company Type | The role the company plays | Distributors, manufacturers, importers |
| Industry/Product | What the company sells or make | Consumer electronics, pet supplements |
| Location | Where the company operates | America, Germany, Southeast Asia, |
| Company Size | How big the company is | 50 to 200 employees |
| Buying Signals | What suggests a current need | Hiring for purchasing roles |

You don't need every part in every prompt. Company type, industry, and location are usually enough to start; add size and buying signals to narrow the results.

## Add buying signals

Buying signals are what make Revor's results different from a plain company database. Instead of every company that fits your profile, you get the ones showing signs of a real need today. Common buying signals include:

- Already carrying a competitor's products
- Hiring for purchasing or sourcing roles
- Expanding production lines
- Frequently attending trade shows
- Building a new factory or opening a new location

Pick the one or two signals most closely tied to buying your product.

## How Revor reads your prompt

When you click **Start Finding Targets**, Revor breaks your prompt into separate criteria, such as industry, location, and company size, and checks each company against them. You can see these criteria in the **Criteria** panel on the right side of your list.

Each part of your prompt becomes something Revor verifies. A vague prompt gives Revor little to check, while a specific one lets it filter out companies that don't fit.

## From vague to specific

| Too vague | Better | Best |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Find electronics companies. | Find consumer electronics distributors in Germany. | Find consumer electronics distributors in Germany with 50 to 500 employees that are currently hiring for purchasing roles. |

The first prompt doesn't say what kind of company or where, so the results will be broad. The last one names the company type, industry, location, size, and a buying signal, so every result is checked against all five.

## More tips

- **Find the right level of detail**<br />More detail isn't always better. Every condition you add, whether it's part of your ICP or a buying signal, is one more thing a company has to match. <br />With too few conditions, results are broad and less accurate. With too many, you may get very few results, or the search may take longer to finish. <br />Start with the conditions that matter most, and add more only if the results are still too broad.
- **Write in any language**<br />Revor supports search prompts in any language, so you can write in the one you're most comfortable with.
- **Describe your customers, not your product**<br />Think about who your customers are, rather than what they buy from you. For example, a company that sells non-slip socks for play areas first searched for:<br />`Find German companies that need non-slip socks.`<br />Few companies can be identified by the socks they need, so the results were limited and not always relevant. The company then described its customers instead:<br />`Find indoor playgrounds in Germany that offer activities such as trampolines.`<br />The results became much more accurate, because indoor playgrounds with trampolines are exactly the businesses that need non-slip socks.
